Browsers want to own your tabs. Switch from Chrome to Safari and your "important reading" pile becomes inaccessible. [Stow](https://github.com/sryo/stow) keeps it in workspaces beside whichever browser you use: the browser hosts the tabs; it doesn't own them.

## What it does

- Attaches to a Chrome, Arc, Safari or Brave window as a sidebar, or runs as its own window.
- Holds links, nested folders, tasks and snippets in workspaces, each with its own colour.
- Clicking a link jumps to the tab if it's already open; otherwise it opens it.
- Imports pinned tabs and folders from Arc, and shares a workspace as a single link.

## How it works

DIAGRAM: diagram.svg "Stow drives the browser from outside, saves locally and syncs its model through your iCloud"

The Mac app follows the front browser window through the Accessibility API and docks beside it. A click asks each running browser over AppleScript whether the tab is open, and focuses it or opens a new one. Every change saves to a local `data.json`, and the app model syncs to the iPhone app through CloudKit.

## Details

- Swift: AppKit on macOS 14, SwiftUI on iOS 17, one shared Foundation-only core
- iPhone app with a share extension and a home-screen widget
- Sync through your own iCloud with `CKSyncEngine`; no account, no server
- Forked from [Arcmark](https://github.com/Geek-1001/arcmark); MIT license
