AirPods have motion sensors and your Mac has accessibility APIs; [AirNod](https://github.com/sryo/AirNod) connects them. I built it for people who can't use a traditional mouse: motor disabilities, repetitive strain injuries, or hands that are simply busy.

## What it does

- Nod up or down, or turn left or right, to scroll. Tilt an ear to your shoulder to click: left clicks, right right-clicks.
- Shake your head to recenter: a three-second countdown, then wherever you're looking becomes the new center.
- Look away from the screen and it pauses; a sudden jolt, like a sneeze, blocks input for a second.
- Lives in the menu bar, where its icon follows your head.

Nod to scroll, tilt an ear to click, shake twice to recenter, look away and it pauses.

## Decisions

### Recenter with a shake

There's a Recenter button in the menu, and a head gesture that does the same.

### Looking away is a pause

Turn your head past the edges of the screen and AirNod stops reading gestures until you look back. The cartoon face in its window closes its eyes while you're away. The limit started as a fixed 45 degrees; in July it became the screen's own edges.
