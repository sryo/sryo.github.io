AirPods have motion sensors and your Mac has accessibility APIs; [AirNod](https://github.com/sryo/AirNod) connects them. I built it for people who can't use a traditional mouse: motor disabilities, repetitive strain injuries, or hands that are simply busy.

## What it does

- Nod up or down, or turn left or right, to scroll. Tilt an ear to your shoulder to click: left clicks, right right-clicks.
- Shake your head to recenter: a three-second countdown, then wherever you're looking becomes the new center.
- Look away from the screen and it pauses; a sudden jolt, like a sneeze, blocks input for a second.
- Lives in the menu bar, with sliders for tilt threshold, scroll deadzone, scroll speed and responsiveness.

## How it works

DIAGRAM: diagram.svg "Head motion comes in over Bluetooth; synthetic mouse events go out to macOS"

Core Motion streams the AirPods' attitude. AirNod smooths it against a calibrated center, then small, unit-tested state machines decide: a nod or turn past the deadzone scrolls, an ear tilt past the threshold clicks, a double shake recenters. Clicks and scrolls are posted as CGEvents, and a looping silent sound keeps the motion session alive.

## Details

- Swift and SwiftUI menu bar app for macOS 14, pure Apple frameworks, no dependencies
- Works with AirPods Pro, AirPods 3rd gen, AirPods Max and Beats Fit Pro
- Needs Accessibility permission to post mouse and scroll events
- Multi-monitor mode keeps a separate head calibration for each display
- Open source under its own share-alike, no-liability license
